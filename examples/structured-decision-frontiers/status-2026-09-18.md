# SemIf-style direct decisions and Jev comparison — 2026-09-18

## Bottom line

SemIf is useful to this experiment as a readout method, not as an open Jev
model. On the exact Qwen3.8 27B GGUF and llama.cpp runtime, reading declared
option logits was more accurate, better calibrated, and much faster than asking
the model to generate a JSON probability object. Real Jev 1.13 was measured
separately through OpenRouter's Decisions endpoint.

The first useful local compound is conservative rather than clever. Qwen3.5 4B
handles only cases it classifies as `light`; it escalates both `heavy` and
`abstain` to Qwen3.8. The pair matches Qwen3.8's 88.89% final accuracy while
reducing heavy-model calls from 1.00 to 0.78 per case. It is slower than Qwen3.8
alone, so it earns a heavy-demand/calibration tradeoff, not the latency win.

## Matched result

All figures use the same 18 public cases, three case-major repetitions, one
request at a time. Local models were warm, prefix caching was enabled, and the
two compound components were co-resident on the 64 GB M5.

## What SemIf changes

The direct backend follows SemIf `direct-options-v1` at
[source revision `92cbedc`](https://github.com/TheoLeeCJ/SemIf/tree/92cbedcd3f9255286c741c5fe0935ecd8c6fc9e6):

1. Render the state, criterion, and declared options with single-token letter
   labels.
2. Request one constrained token with thinking disabled.
3. Read the first-position log probability for every allowed letter.
4. Apply softmax only across those declared choices.

These are conditional option scores, not automatically calibrated confidence.
That is why Brier error remains a separate frontier axis. The llama.cpp path
returns all required letter log-probabilities; oMLX 0.6.4 computes logits
internally but does not expose them in its current chat-completion response, so
the measured direct path uses llama.cpp.

## Negative results and limits

- Qwen3.5 4B direct readout is fast but only 55.56% accurate alone. It mostly
  over-abstains, which makes it conservative but not a good final router.
- Its generated System-One form failed on the first case by returning schema
  material rather than the requested probability object. Direct readout removes
  that output-format failure but not the model's semantic errors.
- A more aggressive general routing instruction raised 4B top-1 accuracy to
  77.78% but created 9 unsafe routes in 54 observations. It was rejected and is
  not in the result-of-record catalog.
- Qwen3.8 direct and Jev both missed the refund side-effect and deployment
  sequence cases; Jev also missed the security-secret route. Eighteen distinct
  cases are too few for a production claim.
- Case-major repetitions create exact cache hits on repetitions two and three.
  p95 still captures most first executions, but a later cold-cache and shuffled
  order panel should be reported separately.
- This screen evaluates one routing decision. It does not prove that the chosen
  worker completes the underlying tool task.

## Frontier residents

- **Decision quality × latency:** Qwen3.5 direct is the very-fast/low-quality
  end; Qwen3.8 direct is the high-quality end. Generated Qwen and Jev are
  dominated on this exact two-axis view.
- **Decision quality × calibration:** the local compound and Jev remain. The
  compound has higher accuracy; Jev has lower Brier error.
- **Decision quality × heavy demand:** the local compound and Jev remain.
  This shared frontier admits single and compound candidates, so Qwen3.8 alone
  is correctly dominated by the equal-accuracy pair that makes fewer heavy
  calls.
- **Decision quality × cost:** only Jev is eligible because it is the only
  offering with a defensible measured dollar cost. This is not a claim that it
  is cheaper than local inference.
- **Safe automation:** no candidate clears the 95% autonomous-accuracy gate.
