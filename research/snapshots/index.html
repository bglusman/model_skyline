Variant A · the Tufte ledger default

Every number a skeptical reader might dispute is printed, not hidden in a tooltip: one exact ledger per workload, dominated rows kept visible with their dominators named, and an inline-SVG sparkline of the frontier embedded per table — zero chart libraries.

Best for: verifying any single number at a glance; print-friendly; no JS dependencies beyond the inlined data. 10 ledger tables + ranked model matrix.

Variant B · the annotated staircase

A Pareto frontier drawn as what it is — a staircase: dominant steps labeled directly, dominated models as ghost dots with arrows to the step that kills them, cost expressed as ×-multiples of the cheapest frontier offering, and one computed editorial callout per frontier.

Best for: seeing the shape of the trade and who is closest to the frontier. Pure SVG, no chart library.

Variant C · the budget explorable

Don't show the reader a frontier — let them push on it: pick workload, budget ceiling and taste (cheapest-clearing-floor / best-affordable / per-dollar / log-ratio) and the page computes your personal pick from the same ranked[] rows, with a next-step-up cost narration.

Best for: turning the table into a decision tool; exercises the "visible dominated-but-close" requirement directly (reachable · dominated rows stay in the verdict table).

Variant D · model dossiers

The buyer's real question is “who is this model for?”: one dossier card per model computed bottom-up from its frontier rows — exact x/y per frontier, cost position on a 10×-spaced ruler, nearest rival, and a computed verdict sentence (frontier resident / niche specialist / off-frontier), plus a two-model head-to-head comparator.

Best for: per-model storytelling and A/B comparisons. Zero chart libraries.
